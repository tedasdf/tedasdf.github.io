## Introduction: The Routing Collapse Problem

Scaling Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models increases parameter capacity without increasing per-token FLOPs. However, large MoE systems often suffer from **routing collapse**, where only a few experts receive most tokens while others remain nearly idle.

Traditional solutions introduce auxiliary balancing losses. Loss-Free Balancing (LFB) removes this loss entirely and replaces it with a dynamic feedback mechanism.

---

### 1. The Problem with Auxiliary Load Balancing

Standard MoE training optimizes:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{LM} + \alpha \mathcal{L}_{bal}
$$

Where:

- $\mathcal{L}_{LM}$ is the language modeling loss  
- $\mathcal{L}_{bal}$ enforces load uniformity  
- $\alpha$ is a balancing coefficient  

#### Gradient Interference

The router must:

1. Choose the best expert for each token  
2. Satisfy global balancing constraints  

These objectives can conflict, degrading convergence.

#### Hyperparameter Sensitivity

If $\alpha$ is:

- Too small → routing collapse  
- Too large → perplexity worsens  

Balancing becomes fragile and scale-dependent.

---

### 2. Loss-Free Balancing (LFB)

Instead of adding a balancing loss, LFB modifies routing logits directly using a feedback signal.

Balancing is removed from backpropagation and handled externally.

---

#### Biased Gating Mechanism

Let the raw routing score be:

$$
s_{i,t} = \text{Router}(x_t)
$$

LFB introduces an expert-specific bias $b_i$:

$$
G_{i,t} = s_{i,t} + b_i
$$

Top-$K$ routing is applied on $G_{i,t}$ instead of $s_{i,t}$.

Key property:

- Gradients flow only through $s_{i,t}$  
- $b_i$ is updated outside backpropagation  

This preserves pure language modeling optimization.

---

#### Measuring Expert Load

After routing a batch, define:

$$
c_i = \text{number of tokens assigned to expert } i
$$

Target average load:

$$
\bar{c} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} c_i
$$

Load deviation:

\[
e_i = c_i - \bar{c}
\]

- If $e_i > 0$, expert $i$ is overloaded  
- If $e_i < 0$, expert $i$ is underutilized  

---

#### Bias Update Rule

The bias is updated using a simple feedback rule:

$$
b_i \leftarrow b_i - \gamma \cdot \text{sign}(e_i)
$$

Where:

- $\gamma$ is a small control step  
- The update occurs per batch or per step  
- No gradients are involved  

If an expert is overloaded, its bias decreases.  
If underutilized, its bias increases.

This forms a stable negative feedback loop.

---

### 3. Why It Works

The router learns the true affinity function $s_{i,t}$.

The bias shifts the decision boundary:

$$
\arg\max_i \left( s_{i,t} + b_i \right)
$$

Instead of forcing balance through gradients, LFB adjusts routing thresholds directly.

This decouples:

- **Routing quality** → learned by gradient descent  
- **Load balancing** → enforced by feedback control  

The result is stable expert utilization without degrading language modeling performance.

---

### Final Thoughts

Loss-Free Balancing reframes MoE routing as a control problem instead of a multi-objective optimization problem.

By separating balancing from backpropagation, it eliminates gradient conflict and stabilizes large-scale expert training.

As MoE systems scale, such decoupled control mechanisms may become the default strategy for expert utilization.
